# The carousel's last chapter

At the start of this track, Personalized was a hunch. Since then:

- **02** — you defined "better" as a **metric**: the share of shoppers who click a recommendation, higher is better. One `track()` line, against the same context that evaluated the flag.
- **03** — you wrote the hypothesis down and designed an **experiment**: Bestsellers (control) vs Personalized, 50/50 on the default rule, Bayesian at 90%.
- **04** — you started the iteration, the shop voted with its clicks, and the results table turned a hunch into a probability.
- **06** — you **shipped the winner**: one click stopped the iteration, recorded the decision, and pointed the flag at Personalized for everyone.
- **07** — you handed the decision to a **multi-armed bandit** that reallocates traffic toward whatever is winning, on a schedule, forever.

# The whole journey

Three tracks, one shop, and mostly the same two flags. Every mechanism answered a different question at a different moment:

| Track | Timescale | Mechanism | Question it answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Release** | Before and during a release | Targeting, progressive rollout, guarded rollout, kill switch | *How much damage can this release do?* |
| **Observability** | During and after a release | Error attribution, session replay, autogenerated metrics, guarded rollout on a real signal, adaptive trigger | *How do I know the moment it breaks, and make the fix automatic?* |
| **Iterate** | Over time | Metrics, experiments, shipping the winner, bandits | *Is the new version actually better?* |

The thing to notice is how little was specific to any one of them. A flag around the behavior. An honest signal about whether it's working. Everything else, the rollback, the self-healing, the experiment, the bandit, is LaunchDarkly reading that signal and acting on the flag. Instrument once, flag everything, and release day stops being an event.
